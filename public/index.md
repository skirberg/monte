## Fourteen topics

1. [Data and sampling](https://monte.markets/learn/data.md): Statistics is using data to make a decision. You can't check everyone, so you check a slice (the sample) and let it speak for the whole group (the population).
2. [Where is the middle](https://monte.markets/learn/center.md): Mean is the average. Median is the middle once sorted. Mode is the most common. The big idea: one extreme number drags the mean; the median barely moves.
3. [How spread out is it](https://monte.markets/learn/spread.md): Range is biggest minus smallest. Standard deviation is the typical distance from the average; in finance, risk. Empirical rule: on hill-shaped data, 68% fall within 1 SD, 95% within 2.
4. [Probability rules](https://monte.markets/learn/prob.md): Or: add, then subtract the overlap. And: multiply. Given: count only inside what you were told. Mutually exclusive: can't both happen. Independent: one says nothing about the other.
5. [Random variables and expected value](https://monte.markets/learn/rv.md): A random variable is a number that depends on chance. Its expected value is the long-run average, weighted by probability. Its SD is the risk around that.
6. [Two variables: covariance, correlation, portfolios](https://monte.markets/learn/two.md): Covariance asks whether two things move together. Correlation rescales it from −1 to +1. Mix two assets and any correlation below +1 lowers the mix's risk. That is diversification.
7. [Counting and the binomial](https://monte.markets/learn/count.md): Repeat a yes-or-no try n independent times with the same chance p: the count of yeses is binomial, mean np, variance np(1 − p). Combinations count the ways it can happen.
8. [Poisson: counts per unit](https://monte.markets/learn/poisson.md): Poisson counts events in a fixed window when they arrive independently at a steady average rate λ. Mean and variance both equal λ. Scale λ to the window first.
9. [The normal curve and z](https://monte.markets/learn/normal.md): The normal is the bell. Measure in SDs from the mean, z = (y − μ) / σ, and any normal becomes the standard one. Area under it is probability.
10. [Sampling distributions and the CLT](https://monte.markets/learn/clt.md): Sample means have their own distribution. The Central Limit Theorem says it is near normal for big n, centered on μ, with spread σ / √n (the standard error).
11. [Confidence intervals and sample size](https://monte.markets/learn/ci.md): A confidence interval is your estimate plus or minus a margin: estimate ± critical value × standard error. Use z when σ is known, t when you only have s.
12. [Hypothesis tests](https://monte.markets/learn/test.md): A hypothesis test asks: would this data be surprising if nothing were going on? Assume nothing is (H₀). A small p-value means the data would be rare, so reject H₀.
13. [Simple regression](https://monte.markets/learn/reg.md): Regression fits the line with the smallest squared misses. Slope: how much y moves per unit of x. R²: the share of y's variation explained. t-test: does x help?
14. [Multiple regression](https://monte.markets/learn/mreg.md): Several x's. Each coefficient is one x's effect holding the others fixed. The F-test checks the whole model, each t-test one x. Multicollinearity: two x's saying the same thing.
