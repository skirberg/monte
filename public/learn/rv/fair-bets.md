# Is the bet fair?

> Expected profit is the expected payout minus the cost. Casinos and insurers price every bet so this is negative for you and positive for them.

**Question:** A raffle ticket costs $5. It wins $100 with chance 0.03. Is it worth buying, on average?

- Yes, $100 is 20 times the price: The prize is big but rare. Weigh it by its chance.
- It breaks even: Breaking even needs a $5 expected win. This one is $3.
- **No, you lose $2 per ticket on average** (right answer): Yes. You win 100 × 0.03 = $3 on average and pay $5.

Expected profit is the expected payout minus the cost. Casinos and insurers price every bet so this is negative for you and positive for them.

**Question:** Insurance costs $200 a year and pays $10,000 if the event happens, with chance 0.01. Expected profit for the insurer per policy?

- $200: It also pays out claims: $100 a policy on average.
- −$9,800: That's the year a claim happens. Averaged over policies, it's +$100.
- **$100** (right answer): Yes. It collects $200 and pays out 10,000 × 0.01 = $100 on average.

A bad bet on average can still be a smart buy when the loss would ruin you. That's insurance.
