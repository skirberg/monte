# One value versus an average

> A sample mean has its own distribution, the sampling distribution.

Canonical URL: https://monte.markets/learn/clt/one-versus-average/

License: CC BY 4.0, reuse with credit to monte.markets.

Lesson 1 of 5 in [Sampling distributions and the CLT](https://monte.markets/learn/clt.md).

**Question:** Bottles hold 500 ml on average, SD 20 ml. Which is more likely to be over 508 ml: one bottle, or the average of 25 bottles?

- **One bottle** (right answer): Yes. Averages wander much less than single values. A single bottle over 508 is common; an average of 25 over 508 is rare.
- The average of 25: Highs and lows cancel in an average, so it stays close to 500.
- Equally likely: Same mean, but the average has a much smaller spread.

A sample mean has its own distribution, the sampling distribution. It's centered on the true mean, but it's narrower than the data, because highs and lows cancel.

**Question:** What happens to the spread of sample means as samples get bigger?

- **It shrinks** (right answer): Yes. More values, more cancelling, steadier averages.
- It grows: Bigger samples make averages steadier, not wilder.
- It stays the same: Single values keep their spread; averages tighten.

Single values keep their SD. Averages get steadier the more values you average.
