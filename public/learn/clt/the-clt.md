# The Central Limit Theorem

> The Central Limit Theorem: averages (and sums) of many independent values are close to normal, even when the values themselves aren't.

**Question:** Wait times are skewed: most are short, a few are very long. What shape is the distribution of averages of 50 wait times?

- Flat: Averages pile up around the true mean, not flat.
- **Close to a bell** (right answer): Yes. Averages of many independent values come out close to normal, whatever the original shape.
- Just as skewed: Single wait times stay skewed, but averaging smooths the shape toward a bell.

The Central Limit Theorem: averages (and sums) of many independent values are close to normal, even when the values themselves aren't. It's why the bell shows up everywhere, and why Monte's board makes one.

**Question:** Does a bigger sample make the data themselves more bell-shaped?

- Yes: Collecting more incomes doesn't change how incomes are spread.
- Only above 30: Rules of thumb like 30 are about averages, not the raw data.
- **No, only the averages** (right answer): Yes. Incomes stay skewed however many you collect. It's the average that becomes normal.

More data doesn't make data normal. It makes averages normal.
