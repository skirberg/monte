# The standard error

> The SD of a sample mean is the standard error: SE = σ ÷ √n. It shrinks with the square root of n, not n itself.

**Question:** Bottles have SD 20 ml. What is the SD of the average of 25 bottles?

- 0.8 ml: That divides by 25. Divide by √25.
- 20 ml: That's for one bottle. Averages vary less.
- **4 ml** (right answer): Yes. 20 ÷ √25 = 20 ÷ 5 = 4.

The SD of a sample mean is the standard error: SE = σ ÷ √n. It shrinks with the square root of n, not n itself.

**Question:** You take 4 times as many measurements. How much does the SE shrink?

- To a quarter: That's dividing by n instead of √n.
- Not at all: More data always makes the average steadier.
- **By half** (right answer): Yes. √4 = 2, so the SE halves.

To halve your uncertainty, you need four times the data.
