# Proportions are averages too

> A sample proportion p̂ is an average of 1s and 0s, so the CLT applies. Its SE is √(p(1 − p) ÷ n).

Lesson 5 of 5 in [Sampling distributions and the CLT](https://monte.markets/learn/clt.md).

**Question:** 60% of customers like a new flavor. In random samples of 100, what's the typical share who like it?

- Exactly 60% every time: Samples vary. Some come in at 55%, some at 65%.
- Anywhere from 0% to 100%: With 100 people, the share stays fairly close to 60%.
- **About 60%, give or take about 5 points** (right answer): Yes. SE = √(0.6 × 0.4 ÷ 100) ≈ 0.049.

A sample proportion p̂ is an average of 1s and 0s, so the CLT applies. Its SE is √(p(1 − p) ÷ n).

**Question:** p = 0.5, n = 400. What's the SE of p̂?

- **0.025** (right answer): Yes. √(0.5 × 0.5 ÷ 400) = √0.000625 = 0.025.
- 0.25: That's p(1 − p). Divide by n and take the root.
- 0.00125: That's 0.5 ÷ 400. Use √(p(1 − p) ÷ n).

Proportions behave like averages: centered on the truth, steadier with bigger samples.
