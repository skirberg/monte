# An estimate and its margin

> A confidence interval is estimate ± margin of error. The margin is a critical value times the standard error: for 95%, about 1.96 × SE.

Lesson 1 of 5 in [Confidence intervals and sample size](https://monte.markets/learn/ci.md).

**Question:** A poll says 52%, with a margin of error of 3 points. What range is the true share likely in?

- 52% exactly: A sample gives an estimate. The margin says how far off it could be.
- 50% to 54%: The margin is 3 points on each side.
- **49% to 55%** (right answer): Yes. Estimate plus or minus the margin.

A confidence interval is estimate ± margin of error. The margin is a critical value times the standard error: for 95%, about 1.96 × SE.

**Question:** 25 bottles average 508 ml. σ = 20, so SE = 4. What's the 95% interval for the line's true mean?

- 508 to 516: The interval goes both ways from the estimate.
- **About 500 to 516** (right answer): Yes. 508 ± 1.96 × 4 ≈ 508 ± 7.8.
- About 468 to 548: That uses σ, the spread of single bottles. An interval for the mean uses the SE.

Estimate ± critical value × SE. The SE, not the SD, because it's about the mean.
