# The mean chases the tail

> Skew is a long tail on one side. In right-skewed data (income, house prices, wait times) the mean sits above the median. In left-skewed data it sits below.

Lesson 4 of 5 in [Where is the middle](https://monte.markets/learn/center.md).

**Question:** Most people earn modest incomes and a few earn huge ones. Which is bigger, the mean or the median?

- They're equal: They're equal for symmetric data. Income has a long tail on the high side.
- **The mean** (right answer): Yes. The long tail of high incomes pulls the mean up past the middle.
- The median: The median sits in the middle. The few huge values drag the mean above it.

Skew is a long tail on one side. In right-skewed data (income, house prices, wait times) the mean sits above the median. In left-skewed data it sits below.

**Question:** Exam scores: most students got 85 to 95, a few got 20 to 40. Mean versus median?

- Mean equals median: The few very low scores make the data lopsided.
- **Mean below median** (right answer): Yes. The tail is on the low side, so it drags the mean down. That's left skew.
- Mean above median: The low scores are the tail, so they pull the mean down, not up.

Look at which side the tail is on. The mean moves toward it.
