# Same average, different ride

> The center says where the data sit. Spread says how far they wander. Two data sets can share a mean and feel completely different.

Canonical URL: https://monte.markets/learn/spread/why-spread/

License: CC BY 4.0, reuse with credit to monte.markets.

Lesson 1 of 5 in [How spread out is it](https://monte.markets/learn/spread.md).

**Question:** Two commutes both average 30 minutes. One is always 28 to 32, the other is 10 to 60. Which would you rather take?

- **The 28 to 32 one** (right answer): Yes. Same average, but you can plan around it. That steadiness is low spread.
- The 10 to 60 one: Some days it's quick, but you can never count on it.
- No difference, same average: The average hides how much each day swings.

The center says where the data sit. Spread says how far they wander. Two data sets can share a mean and feel completely different.

**Question:** Two funds both return 7% a year on average. Fund A swings from 5% to 9%; fund B from −20% to 30%. Which has more risk?

- Fund A: Fund A barely moves. Fund B's years land all over the place.
- Same risk, same average: The average is the same; the spread is not.
- **Fund B** (right answer): Yes. Risk in finance is spread: how far a year can land from the average.

Always report a spread next to an average. Alone, an average can hide almost anything.
