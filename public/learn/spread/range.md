# The range sees only the ends

> The range is the largest value minus the smallest. It's quick, but it only looks at two values and ignores everything between them. The range is a first look.

Lesson 2 of 5 in [How spread out is it](https://monte.markets/learn/spread.md).

**Question:** What is the range of 4, 9, 15, 2, 11?

- 11: That's the last value. The range is max minus min.
- 8.2: That's the mean. The range is about the extremes.
- **13** (right answer): Yes. Largest minus smallest: 15 − 2 = 13.

The range is the largest value minus the smallest. It's quick, but it only looks at two values and ignores everything between them.

**Question:** Lists 10, 50, 50, 50, 90 and 10, 10, 50, 90, 90 have the same range. Same spread?

- Yes, the range is 80 for both: The range can't see how the values between the ends are spread.
- Only if the means match: Both means are 50, and the spreads still differ.
- **No** (right answer): Yes, right: the first huddles in the middle, the second sits at the edges. Their SDs are about 28 and 40.

The range is a first look. For a real measure of spread, use one that sees every value: the standard deviation.
