# How many SDs away

> A z-score counts how many SDs a value is from the mean: z = (value − mean) ÷ SD. Positive is above the mean, negative below.

Lesson 5 of 5 in [How spread out is it](https://monte.markets/learn/spread.md).

**Question:** A test has mean 70 and SD 5. You scored 80. How unusual is that?

- **2 SDs above the mean** (right answer): Yes. (80 − 70) ÷ 5 = 2. That's a z-score of 2.
- 10 points above, so very unusual: 10 points could be a lot or a little. It depends on the spread.
- About average: 80 is well above the 70 average.

A z-score counts how many SDs a value is from the mean: z = (value − mean) ÷ SD. Positive is above the mean, negative below. It lets you compare things on different scales.

**Question:** You got 80 on a test with mean 70 and SD 5, and 85 on one with mean 80 and SD 10. Which was the stronger result?

- **The first** (right answer): Yes. The first is z = 2; the second is z = 0.5. Same student, very different standing.
- The second, 85 is higher: The raw score ignores how the class did. Compare z-scores.
- They're equal: Their z-scores are 2 and 0.5.

To compare across scales, turn values into z-scores. Beyond about 2 in either direction counts as unusual.
