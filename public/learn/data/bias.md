# Bigger isn't fairer

> Bias is a sample that leans one way every time. More data from the same leaning source doesn't fix it. That's why a careful 1,000 beats a careless 80,000.

Lesson 4 of 5 in [Data and sampling](https://monte.markets/learn/data.md).

**Question:** A news site's online poll gets 80,000 votes. A survey firm calls 1,000 random adults. Which tells you more about all adults?

- They tell you the same: They measure different groups. Only the random one is aimed at all adults.
- **The 1,000 random adults** (right answer): Yes. 80,000 self-selected votes measure who visits that site and bothers to vote.
- The 80,000 votes, it's 80 times bigger: Size cuts random noise, not bias. A huge leaning sample is just confidently wrong.

Bias is a sample that leans one way every time. More data from the same leaning source doesn't fix it. That's why a careful 1,000 beats a careless 80,000.

**Question:** A gym surveys members during the 7 am rush. What could bias it?

- **It only reaches early risers** (right answer): Yes. People who come at night or on weekends never get asked.
- The sample is too small: Small samples are noisy, but the bigger worry is who's missing completely.
- Nothing, they're all members: Being a member isn't enough. They're one kind of member.

Before trusting a number, ask who could never have ended up in the sample.
