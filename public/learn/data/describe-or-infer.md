# Describe or infer

> Describing summarizes the data you have. Inferring uses a sample to say something about a bigger group you didn't fully see.

Canonical URL: https://monte.markets/learn/data/describe-or-infer/

License: CC BY 4.0, reuse with credit to monte.markets.

Lesson 2 of 5 in [Data and sampling](https://monte.markets/learn/data.md).

**Question:** A coach says: "My team's average age is 24." Is that describing or inferring?

- **Describing** (right answer): Yes. The coach has every player's age and is just summarizing them.
- Inferring: Inferring would mean using some players to guess about a bigger group. The coach has the whole team.
- Neither: A summary of data you have is describing. That's exactly this.

Describing summarizes the data you have. Inferring uses a sample to say something about a bigger group you didn't fully see. Polls, trials and A/B tests are all inference.

**Question:** A poll of 1,000 voters says 52% will vote yes. Describing or inferring?

- **Inferring** (right answer): Yes. 1,000 people are being used to speak for millions of voters.
- Describing: It would be describing if the 52% were only about those 1,000. The poll means everyone.
- Describing, because it's a percent: A percent can do either job. What matters is whether it's meant to reach past the people measured.

Ask who the number is about. If it's about more people than were measured, it's inference, and it carries uncertainty.
