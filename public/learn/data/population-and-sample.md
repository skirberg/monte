# The pot and the spoonful

> The population is everyone the question is about. The sample is the part you actually measure.

Lesson 1 of 5 in [Data and sampling](https://monte.markets/learn/data.md).

**Question:** A coffee chain wants to know how all 40,000 of its customers rate the new cup. It asks 400. Which group is the population?

- Every coffee drinker anywhere: The chain only asked about its own customers, so that's the group the answer is for.
- **The 40,000 customers** (right answer): Yes. The population is everyone you want an answer about. You rarely get to ask all of them.
- The 400 asked: Those 400 are the sample: the slice you actually ask. The question is about all 40,000.

The population is everyone the question is about. The sample is the part you actually measure. Statistics is using the sample to speak for the population, like tasting a spoonful to judge the pot.

**Question:** A school measures the height of 30 of its 600 pupils. What is n, the sample size?

- **30** (right answer): Yes. Small n is the sample size. Capital N, 600, is the population size.
- 600: That's N, the population. The sample is the 30 measured.
- 570: That's the pupils not measured. n counts the ones you have data on.

Keep the two apart: the number from the sample is an estimate, and the number for the whole population is the one you're after.
