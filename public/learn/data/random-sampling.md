# Fair picks

> A simple random sample gives everyone the same chance of being chosen.

Canonical URL: https://monte.markets/learn/data/random-sampling/

License: CC BY 4.0, reuse with credit to monte.markets.

Lesson 3 of 5 in [Data and sampling](https://monte.markets/learn/data.md).

**Question:** You want to know what members of a gym think. Which sample is fairest?

- **Pick 150 names at random from the full member list** (right answer): Yes. Every member has the same chance of being picked, so no group is favored.
- Ask the first 150 people through the door on Monday: Monday-morning regulars aren't every member. Night and weekend people get left out.
- Post a survey and use whoever answers: People who answer surveys tend to feel strongly. That leans the result.

A simple random sample gives everyone the same chance of being chosen. A stratified sample splits people into groups first (by age, by size of claim) and picks at random inside each, so every group shows up.

**Question:** An auditor has 50,000 expense claims, a few of them huge. Which sample catches the risk best?

- 300 claims at random from all of them: Fair, but with only a few huge claims, a random 300 may miss them all.
- The 300 most recent claims: Recent isn't random. A month-end rush could tilt everything.
- **Split by amount, pick at random in each group, and take extra big claims** (right answer): Yes. That's stratified and risk-based: every size shows up, and the big ones, where the money is, get a closer look.

Random keeps you fair. Stratifying makes sure the groups you care about are in the sample.
