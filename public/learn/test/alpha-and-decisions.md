# Deciding with α

> α is the cutoff you choose in advance, often 0.05. If p ≤ α, reject H₀. If p > α, fail to reject.

Lesson 3 of 5 in [Hypothesis tests](https://monte.markets/learn/test.md).

**Question:** You set α = 0.05 and get p = 0.03. What do you do?

- Accept H₀: p below α means reject. And you never "accept" H₀, you just fail to reject it.
- Raise α: Choose α before you look at the data.
- **Reject H₀** (right answer): Yes. p is below α, so the data are rare enough under H₀ to reject it.

α is the cutoff you choose in advance, often 0.05. If p ≤ α, reject H₀. If p > α, fail to reject. Failing to reject isn't proof that H₀ is true, just not enough evidence against it.

**Question:** p = 0.12 with α = 0.05. What can you say?

- Reject H₀: 0.12 is above 0.05.
- **Not enough evidence to reject H₀** (right answer): Yes. It doesn't prove no effect, it just isn't strong evidence for one.
- H₀ is true: Absence of evidence isn't proof. A bigger sample might show an effect.

Reject or fail to reject. Never accept.
