# The p-value

> The p-value is the chance of data at least as extreme as yours, assuming H₀ is true.

Lesson 2 of 5 in [Hypothesis tests](https://monte.markets/learn/test.md).

**Question:** A test gives p = 0.03. What does that mean?

- **If H₀ were true, data this extreme would turn up 3% of the time** (right answer): Yes. It's how surprising the data would be if nothing were going on.
- There's a 3% chance H₀ is true: The p-value assumes H₀ is true. It can't also be the chance H₀ is true.
- There's a 97% chance the effect is real: Same mistake turned around.

The p-value is the chance of data at least as extreme as yours, assuming H₀ is true. Small p means your data would be rare under H₀, which is evidence against it.

**Question:** Which p-value is stronger evidence against H₀?

- 0.2: 1 in 5 is not surprising.
- 0.5: Data like this would happen half the time anyway.
- **0.001** (right answer): Yes. The smaller the p-value, the more surprising the data would be under H₀.

Small p: surprising under H₀. It's never the chance that H₀ is true.
