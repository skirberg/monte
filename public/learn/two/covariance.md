# Do they move together?

> Covariance says whether two variables move together (positive), in opposite directions (negative), or neither (near zero).

**Question:** On hot days an ice cream shop sells more. Temperature and sales tend to...

- Have nothing to do with each other: Sales clearly climb with the heat.
- **Rise together** (right answer): Yes. High with high, low with low. That's positive covariance.
- Move in opposite directions: That would be negative covariance, like temperature and coat sales.

Covariance says whether two variables move together (positive), in opposite directions (negative), or neither (near zero). Its size depends on the units, so on its own it's hard to read.

**Question:** Hours of TV watched and exam scores tend to go in opposite directions. Covariance is...

- Positive: Positive means they rise together. These move opposite.
- Zero: There is a pattern here, just a downward one.
- **Negative** (right answer): Yes. When one is high the other tends to be low.

Covariance gives you the direction. To judge strength, you need correlation.
