# Why mixing lowers risk

> Portfolio variance is b²V(X) + c²V(Y) + 2bc·Cov(X, Y).

**Question:** Two stocks each have an SD of 20%. They move in opposite directions (correlation −1). Half in each: what's the risk?

- 40%: Risks don't add like that, especially when they offset.
- **0%** (right answer): Yes. When one falls, the other rises by the same amount, so the swings cancel exactly.
- 20%: That would be true if they moved together. Opposite moves cancel.

Portfolio variance is b²V(X) + c²V(Y) + 2bc·Cov(X, Y). The covariance term is the key: when assets don't move together, it's small or negative, and the mix is less risky than its parts.

**Question:** Which pair lowers risk most when mixed?

- Two assets with correlation 0.9: They move almost together, so mixing barely helps.
- Two assets with correlation 1: Perfectly together means no cancelling at all.
- **Two assets with correlation −0.5** (right answer): Yes. The more they move opposite, the more their swings cancel.

Diversifying works because of covariance. Mix things that don't move in step.
