# Mixing two investments

> A portfolio's expected return is the weighted average of the parts: E(bX + cY) = b·E(X) + c·E(Y). The weights are the shares of money.

**Question:** You put half your money in a fund returning 6% and half in one returning 10%. Expected return?

- **8%** (right answer): Yes. 0.5 × 6 + 0.5 × 10 = 8.
- 16%: That adds them. A portfolio averages them by weight.
- 10%: Only half your money earns 10%.

A portfolio's expected return is the weighted average of the parts: E(bX + cY) = b·E(X) + c·E(Y). The weights are the shares of money.

**Question:** 70% in a fund expecting 4%, 30% in one expecting 14%. Expected return?

- **7%** (right answer): Yes. 0.7 × 4 + 0.3 × 14 = 2.8 + 4.2 = 7.
- 9%: That's the plain average. Weight by how much money is in each.
- 18%: Returns don't add up across funds. They average by weight.

Returns average by weight. Risk is where it gets interesting.
