# Together isn't because

> Correlation shows that two things move together. It doesn't show that one causes the other. A hidden third factor often drives both.

Lesson 3 of 5 in [Two variables: covariance, correlation, portfolios](https://monte.markets/learn/two.md).

**Question:** Towns with more firefighters have more fire damage. Do firefighters cause damage?

- Yes, the numbers move together: Moving together doesn't say which causes which, or whether something else drives both.
- Only in big towns: Town size isn't the point. Fire size causes both the crews and the damage.
- **No, bigger fires bring more firefighters** (right answer): Yes. A third thing, fire size, drives both.

Correlation shows that two things move together. It doesn't show that one causes the other. A hidden third factor often drives both. To show cause, you need an experiment.

**Question:** Y = X² for X from −3 to 3. Y is completely determined by X. What's the correlation?

- −1: There's no overall straight-line trend.
- **0** (right answer): Yes. Correlation only measures straight-line links. This curve goes down, then up, and the halves cancel.
- 1: Y depends fully on X, but not in a straight line.

Zero correlation means no straight-line link, not no link. Always look at the scatter plot.
