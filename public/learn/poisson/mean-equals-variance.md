# Mean and variance match

> A Poisson count has mean λ and variance λ, so SD = √λ. If real counts vary much more than their mean, the Poisson model doesn't fit. Mean λ, SD √λ.

**Question:** For a Poisson count with λ = 9, what is the SD?

- **3** (right answer): Yes. The variance equals λ = 9, so the SD is √9 = 3.
- 9: That's the variance. Take the square root.
- 4.5: The SD is √λ, not half of it.

A Poisson count has mean λ and variance λ, so SD = √λ. If real counts vary much more than their mean, the Poisson model doesn't fit.

**Question:** A shop says 25 customers an hour on average. Roughly how much do hourly counts swing?

- They don't swing: Counts vary hour to hour around the average.
- **About ±5 around 25** (right answer): Yes. SD = √25 = 5.
- About ±25: That would be SD = 25. For Poisson, SD = √λ.

Mean λ, SD √λ. Bigger rates swing more in total, but less relative to their size.
