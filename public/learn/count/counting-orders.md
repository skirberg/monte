# How many ways

> When a choice is made in stages, multiply the number of options at each stage. Arranging n different things in order has n! ways (n × (n − 1) × ... × 1).

Lesson 1 of 5 in [Counting and the binomial](https://monte.markets/learn/count.md).

**Question:** A café has 3 breads and 4 fillings. How many different sandwiches?

- 7: That adds. For one of each, multiply.
- 24: That's 4 × 3 × 2, which counts orderings. Here it's bread times filling.
- **12** (right answer): Yes. Each of 3 breads goes with each of 4 fillings: 3 × 4.

When a choice is made in stages, multiply the number of options at each stage. Arranging n different things in order has n! ways (n × (n − 1) × ... × 1).

**Question:** How many ways can 4 runners finish first to fourth?

- 16: That's 4 × 4. Once someone finishes, they can't finish again.
- 4: That's how many can win. You want every full order.
- **24** (right answer): Yes. 4 choices for first, 3 for second, 2 for third, 1 for fourth: 4! = 24.

Stages multiply. Each pick that uses something up leaves one fewer for the next.
