# Either one: don't count the overlap twice

> Addition rule: P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A and B). If A and B can't happen together (mutually exclusive), the overlap is 0 and you just add.

**Question:** In a class, 60% play a sport, 40% play music, and 20% do both. What share do at least one?

- 60%: That leaves out the music-only students.
- **80%** (right answer): Yes. 60 + 40 counts the 20% who do both twice, so take it off once.
- 100%: That counts the people who do both twice.

Addition rule: P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A and B). If A and B can't happen together (mutually exclusive), the overlap is 0 and you just add.

**Question:** A card is drawn from a deck. P(heart or king)?

- 17/52: The king of hearts got counted twice.
- 13/52: That's just the hearts.
- **16/52** (right answer): Yes. 13 hearts + 4 kings − 1 king of hearts = 16.

Before adding chances, ask what sits in both. Take that off once.
