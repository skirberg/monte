# Independent or exclusive

> Independent means knowing one tells you nothing about the other: P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B). Mutually exclusive means they can't happen together.

**Question:** A die shows 1 or 6. Can it show both at once? And if you know it's 1, what's the chance it's 6?

- Yes, and 1/6: A single roll shows one face.
- **No, and 0** (right answer): Yes. They're mutually exclusive, and knowing one tells you the other didn't happen.
- No, and still 1/6: Once you know it's 1, a 6 is impossible.

Independent means knowing one tells you nothing about the other: P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B). Mutually exclusive means they can't happen together. Those are nearly opposites.

**Question:** P(A) = 0.5, P(B) = 0.4, P(A and B) = 0.2. Independent?

- **Yes** (right answer): Yes. 0.5 × 0.4 = 0.2, exactly the chance of both.
- No, they overlap: Overlapping is fine. Independence is about whether the overlap equals the product.
- Can't tell: You can: check whether P(A) × P(B) equals P(A and B).

Test independence by multiplying. If the product matches the chance of both, they're independent.
